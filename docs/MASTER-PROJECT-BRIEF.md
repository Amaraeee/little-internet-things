
# LITTLE INTERNET THINGS
## Master Project Brief

### 1. About the Studio

Little Internet Things is a creative digital gifting studio.

We create personalised websites and interactive digital experiences for birthdays, relationships, friendships, graduations, anniversaries and other meaningful occasions.

Our philosophy:

A whole little world, made for someone.

We are not a generic website development agency. We create thoughtful, imaginative digital experiences that make people feel something.

### 2. Creative Direction

The six templates must have DISTINCT visual identities.

The studio's shared identity comes through:
- Thoughtful creative direction.
- High-quality interactions.
- Excellent mobile experiences.
- Playful internet-inspired creativity.
- Editorial design and immersive storytelling.

Do not make all six templates look like recoloured versions of one website.

### 3. The Six Products

1. THE BIRTHDAY SALON
A vintage-inspired interactive birthday celebration.

2. DEAR, MY LOVE
A romantic digital love letter with envelopes, memories and hidden messages.

3. OUR LITTLE ARCHIVE
A digital museum preserving shared memories, photographs and inside jokes.

4. THE NEXT CHAPTER
An editorial graduation and achievement magazine.

5. THE APPRECIATION CLUB
A playful Y2K-inspired internet fan club dedicated to someone special.

6. THE LITTLE CINEMA
A cinematic experience presenting a person's life or relationship as a film.

Each product requires its own:
- Creative brief.
- Visual identity.
- Page architecture.
- Interaction map.
- Typography and colour system.
- Responsive layout.
- Testing checklist.

### 4. Technical Standards

Use HTML, CSS and JavaScript unless a different approach is justified.

Prioritise:
- Mobile-first development.
- Compatibility with Android and iPhone.
- GitHub Pages compatibility.
- Relative asset paths.
- Lightweight animations.
- Accessible typography and contrast.
- Reusable components where appropriate.
- Clear and organised code.
- Fast loading on mobile data.

Every visible button must work.

Never create fake interactions or placeholder controls that appear functional.

Do not autoplay music. Provide clear user-controlled audio controls.

Provide accessible alternatives for interactions that require microphones or other device permissions.

### 5. Customer Personalisation

Templates must support replacing:
- Recipient names.
- Photographs.
- Messages and letters.
- Dates.
- Memories.
- Colours where appropriate.
- Audio and other authorised media.

Keep customer content separate from reusable template design wherever practical.

Never place private customer information, credentials, API keys or unauthorised photographs in a public repository.

### 6. Development Workflow

For every template:

1. Present the creative direction.
2. Design the experience architecture.
3. Map all interactions.
4. Propose the file structure.
5. Identify reusable components.
6. Wait for approval before implementing.
7. Build in manageable stages.
8. Test all interactions.
9. Check mobile responsiveness.
10. Prepare customer customisation instructions.

Do not generate all six websites in one response.

Do not sacrifice quality to reduce development time.

### 7. Collaboration Rules

The founder is not an experienced programmer.

Explain technical decisions in accessible language.

Provide complete replacement files when code changes are necessary.

Do not assume the founder knows how to debug or deploy code.

Avoid unnecessary dependencies and paid services.

Explain any important limitations before implementation.

### 8. Quality Standard

Every finished website should feel like a complete, intentional digital experience.

It should have:
- A memorable opening.
- A coherent journey.
- Meaningful interactions.
- A satisfying ending.
- Excellent mobile presentation.
- Replaceable customer content.
- Reliable deployment.

The objective is to create products customers would genuinely be excited to purchase and share.
